1213. Array subscripting and xvalues
Section: 7.6.1.2 [expr.sub [1]] Status: CD3 Submitter: Jason Merrill Date: 2010-10-24

[Moved to DR status at the April, 2013 meeting.]

Because the subscripting operation is defined as indirection through a pointer value,
the result of a subscript operator applied to an xvalue array is an lvalue, not an
xvalue. This could be surprising to some.

Proposed resolution (December, 2012):

Change 7.6.1.2 [expr.sub [2]] paragraph 1 as follows:

A postfix expression followed by an expression in square brackets is a postfix
expression. One of the expressions shall have the type [INSERT: “array of T” or]
“pointer to T” and the other shall have unscoped enumeration or integral type. The
result is [DELETE: an lvalue] of type “T.” The type “T” shall be a completely-defined
object type.62 The expression E1[E2] is identical (by definition) to *((E1)+(E2)) [Note:
see 7.6.2 [expr.unary [3]] and 7.6.6 [expr.add [4]] for details of * and + and 9.3.4.5
[dcl.array [5]] for details of arrays. —end note][INSERT: , except that in the case of
an array operand, the result is an lvalue if that operand is an lvalue and an xvalue
otherwise.]

(See also issue 616 [6].)

Additional note (January, 2013):

The preceding resolution differs from that discussed in the December, 2012 drafting
review teleconference by the deletion of the words “an lvalue” in the second sentence.
The issue has consequently been moved to "review" status instead of "tentatively ready."
